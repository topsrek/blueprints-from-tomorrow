Why this era?

Vienna, residence of Emperor Leopold I. An Ottoman army under Grand Vizier Kara Mustafa is marching up the Danube. The fortifications have been neglected for decades, the plague of 1679 is four years past, and the court is preparing to leave. On 14 July the city is surrounded. For two months some sixteen thousand soldiers and citizens hold the walls while miners dig towards each other in the dark, dysentery empties the companies, and everyone watches the hills for a relief army.

Unlike Florence, this is a world that already knows what an experiment is. Guericke showed the vacuum to the Imperial Diet thirty years ago; telescopes, microscopes, barometers and pendulum clocks exist; Leibniz and Newton are at work. But Vienna reads about the new philosophy more than it practises it, and a siege leaves little patience for anything that does not help this week.

How people think here

Vienna is the residence of Emperor Leopold I and a city of the Counter-Reformation: the university is run by the Jesuits, books are censored, the Jews were expelled in 1670 and Protestants are barely tolerated. Learned medicine is still Galenic, with chemical remedies and the circulation of the blood accepted by some; plague (the city lost many thousands in 1679) is explained by corrupted air, contagion through goods and persons, and God's punishment for sin. Astronomy is taught after Tycho, not Copernicus. The new experimental philosophy of London, Paris and the Dutch towns (air pump, barometer, microscope, pendulum clock) is known here from Latin books and Jesuit compendia rather than practised. Craft knowledge is strong: gun founders, clockmakers, apothecaries, miners from the Habsburg lands. In June 1683 an Ottoman army is marching on the city, whose fortifications have been neglected.

Significant events around this time

  • 1679: Great plague in Vienna; the court leaves, Sorbait is made Generalinquisitor, Leopold I vows a column on the Graben. Modern estimates of the dead start at about 12,000; contemporaries claimed far more (background).
  • 1680: Sorbait's Pest-Ordnung and Abraham a Sancta Clara's Mercks Wienn are printed (background).
  • 1680–1682 (background): the great comet of 1680–1681 and the comet of 1682 (later Halley's) are read in Vienna as omens of war.
  • 1682 (background): Imre Thököly, with Ottoman backing, holds Upper Hungary; Caprara's embassy to prolong the Peace of Vasvár fails.
  • 1682 (background): Acta Eruditorum, the first German learned journal, begins at Leipzig; Leibniz prints his series for pi in it.
  • 1683: Jakob Bernoulli in Basel studies compound interest reckoned ever more often (the limit later called e).
  • 1683: Blondel's L'art de jetter les bombes, on gunnery by Galileo's parabola, appears in Paris.
  • 1683 Jan 26: defensive alliance of Leopold I with Elector Max Emanuel of Bavaria.
  • 1683 Mar 31: alliance of Leopold I and Jan III Sobieski, dated 31 March (signed at Warsaw on 1 April), with papal backing: each helps if Vienna or Kraków is besieged. Days later the Ottoman army leaves Adrianople.
  • 1683 Jul 7: cavalry clash at Petronell; that evening the emperor and court leave Vienna for Linz and Passau.
  • 1683 Jul 9: Leopold appoints a college of deputies under Kaplirs to govern Vienna.
  • 1683 Jul 10–13: about 11,000 imperial troops enter the city; some 5,000 citizens and volunteers join them.
