Why this era?

Florence at the height of the Renaissance. Lorenzo de' Medici rules a republic that isn't one. Ficino translates Plato, Botticelli paints, the boy Michelangelo carves in the Medici garden. And in the narrow streets of the Oltrarno, the plague smoulders again. A Dominican friar named Savonarola has just begun to preach that God's punishment is coming.

How people think here

Medicine follows Galen and Avicenna: disease as imbalance of the four humours, plague from corrupted air (miasma) and unfavourable planetary conjunctions. Natural philosophy follows Aristotle (nature abhors a vacuum, four elements). Neoplatonism is fashionable at the Medici court (Ficino). Astrology is respected science. Workshops (botteghe), guilds, hospitals and the Studio at Pisa all train specialists. Florence is a republic in name, ruled in practice by Lorenzo de' Medici. Savonarola returns to San Marco in 1490.

Significant events around this time

  • 1488 Dec (background): Dias returns to Lisbon after rounding southern Africa. News reaches mapmakers in Florence.
  • 1490: Regiomontanus' Tabulae directionum (with a tangent table) printed at Augsburg
  • 1490 Jun: Leonardo and Francesco di Giorgio Martini travel together from Milan to Pavia to advise on the cathedral
  • 1490 Aug: After returning to Florence, Savonarola begins a series of sermons at San Marco.
  • 1490: Mercury is discovered at Idrija in Carniola. Almadén is already an established source.
  • c. 1490: Columbus continues seeking Castilian backing. Advisers examining the proposed westward route doubt its feasibility.
  • 1490: Maximilian I hires the Tassis family to run a relay post from Innsbruck to the Netherlands
  • 1490: the first printed portolano (sailing directions) appears in Venice
  • c. 1490–91: Pêro da Covilhã returns to Cairo with information on Indian Ocean trade. Whether his report reached João II is uncertain.
  • 1491: The illustrated Fasciculus medicinae appears in Venice. An expanded Italian edition follows in 1494.
  • 1491 Nov–1492 Mar: Amerigo Vespucci moves from Florence to Seville. He is a witness there on 10 March 1492.
  • 1491 Dec: Bertoldo di Giovanni dies at Poggio a Caiano
