Why this era?

The same city as in Florence 1490, 143 years earlier and without a printing press. Florence is worn out: the harvest of 1346 failed and thousands died in the famine of spring 1347, and the great banks of the Bardi and Peruzzi have collapsed. In the same weeks, far to the south, Genoese galleys from the Black Sea put in at Messina with dying crews. By spring the sickness will be in Florence, and by autumn about half the city will be dead.

This is the game's hygiene and epidemic test in its purest form. The player knows what is coming and when. Nobody else does, and nobody has a reason to believe a stranger.

How people think here

Medicine follows Galen and Avicenna as taught at Bologna, Padua and Montpellier: disease is an imbalance of the four humours; pestilence comes from corrupted air (miasma) stirred up by planetary conjunctions, earthquakes and God's anger at sin. Natural philosophy is scholastic Aristotle. Astrology is respected science. There is no printing: knowledge travels by sermon, council decree, notary, letter and copyist, and most people cannot read Latin. Florence is a guild republic of perhaps 90,000 to 100,000 people, governed since 1343 by priors drawn by lot from the guilds, shaken by the collapse of the Bardi and Peruzzi banks and by the famine of 1346–47. The pope sits at Avignon. Practical knowledge lives in workshops and guilds; merchants reckon with Arabic numerals in abacus schools but keep formal ledgers in Roman numerals.

Significant events around this time

  • 1346 Jan: the Bardi company fails (January 1345 in Florentine reckoning), after the Peruzzi (1343); credit and employment in the city collapse.
  • 1346: plague breaks out in the Mongol army besieging Genoese Caffa in the Crimea.
  • 1346 Mar: the Franciscan inquisitor Piero dell'Aquila, collecting a cardinal's debt from the failed Acciaiuoli, has citizens seized; a crowd frees one. He excommunicates the priors and lays an interdict; the commune curbs his powers and the clergy's armed retinues.
  • 1346 Aug 26: Crécy. English archers and dismounted men-at-arms defeat French cavalry and Genoese crossbowmen; Villani reports English bombards.
  • 1346 Sep–1347 Aug: Edward III besieges Calais for eleven months; it surrenders from hunger.
  • 1346 autumn: harvest fails across Tuscany after months of rain.
  • 1347 Feb: Genoa founds the Maona of Chios, repaying the shipowners of its 1346 fleet with shares in the island's revenues.
  • 1347 Apr: William of Ockham dies at Munich.
  • 1347 mid-Apr: famine at its worst. The commune bakes bread in its own ovens and rations it by ticket, two loaves a mouth; Villani counts 94,000 mouths.
  • 1347 May 20: Cola di Rienzo seizes power in Rome as tribune; he abdicates and flees on Dec 15.
  • 1347 summer: sickness follows the famine; Villani reports more than 4,000 dead in Florence, mostly women and children.
  • 1347 Aug: the commune issues a new, worse silver coin, the guelfo of five soldi; a comet is seen, which Villani records as a sign.
