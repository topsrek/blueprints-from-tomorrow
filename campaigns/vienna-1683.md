# Campaign: Vienna 1683 – The Siege

- **era guide:** `techtree/generated/europe-1683-vienna.md` (compact fallback: `techtree/generated/europe-1683-vienna/briefing.md`; source: `techtree/eras/europe-1683-vienna.yaml`)
- **start date:** 20 June 1683 (adjustable)
- **hook:** accident (default) or mission from the future (option below)
- **recommended duration:** one year (the siege falls in months 1–3), or "one month"
  starting 14 July for a short, hard game

## Premise
Vienna, residence of Emperor Leopold I. An Ottoman army under Grand Vizier Kara
Mustafa is marching up the Danube. The fortifications have been neglected for
decades, the plague of 1679 is four years past, and the court is preparing to leave.
On 14 July the city is surrounded. For two months some sixteen thousand soldiers and
citizens hold the walls while miners dig towards each other in the dark, dysentery
empties the companies, and everyone watches the hills for a relief army.

Unlike Florence, this is a world that already knows what an experiment is. Guericke
showed the vacuum to the Imperial Diet thirty years ago; telescopes, microscopes,
barometers and pendulum clocks exist; Leibniz and Newton are at work. But Vienna
reads about the new philosophy more than it practises it, and a siege leaves little
patience for anything that does not help this week.

## Mission from the future (optional hook)
The message reads: *"The city holds until 12 September, barely. Thousands inside die
of disease, not of the enemy. You will be returned in one year."*

## Opening scene
The player comes to on the Glacis, the open ground before the walls, among carts and
labourers. Hundreds of men are digging and hammering palisades into the counterscarp
under shouted orders; an engineer with a rolled plan curses the state of a ravelin.
Refugees from Hungary push towards the gates with their cattle. Someone asks, in
German, which company the stranger belongs to.

## Arc (loose, react to the player)
1. **Three weeks** – before the gates close. Shelter, papers, a patron: the city
   council, the university, a monastery, an apothecary, the arsenal, an officer.
   Anything the player needs from outside (materials, letters, a way out) must be
   arranged now.
2. **The court leaves** (7 July), the suburbs are burned, the city is surrounded
   (14 July). Whoever stays is committed.
3. **The siege** – the walls, the mines, hunger and the "red flux". The player's
   knowledge is tested where it matters most: water and latrines, wounds, food
   stores, fire, signalling to the relief army, listening for miners.
4. **The crisis** – early September: breaches at the Burg bastion, rockets from
   St Stephen's tower calling for help.
5. **Relief** – 12 September, the battle at the Kahlenberg.
6. **After** – typhus in the autumn, a city in ruins, an emperor returning, and for
   the first time people with leisure to listen. The long part of the year: the
   university, the court, letters to Leibniz, Huygens or the Royal Society, the war
   moving into Hungary.

## Hard choices
- **Leave or stay.** A stranger without a duty can still walk out in early July.
- **Help that kills.** Almost everything useful in a siege is also a weapon, and the
  people on the other side of the wall are people. The game does not hide that the
  aftermath is a long war of conquest in Hungary.
- **Who gets the knowledge.** The court, the Church, the city, or the republic of
  letters abroad? What reaches Leibniz by post may change more than what is said to
  a general.
- **Suspicion.** A stranger with strange objects in a besieged city is a spy before
  he is a sorcerer. Spies are hanged.

## Medium-difficulty hint examples (incidental, never highlighted)
- A water carrier grumbles that since the conduit from outside was cut, everyone
  draws from the house wells, "even the ones by the privies".
- A surgeon's mate notes that the men quartered in the monastery cellars sicken
  faster than those on the walls.
- A citizen-soldier tells how a drum with dried peas on its skin was set in a cellar
  and the peas danced before the mine went off.
- An apothecary mentions a learned Dutchman who claims to have seen little animals in
  rainwater, "but he will show nobody his glasses".

## People specific to this campaign (fictional; real people are in the era file)
- **Frau Barbara Leitnerin**, baker's widow near the Hoher Markt – feeds a company of
  the citizen guard, knows which wells have gone bad.
- **Feldscher Matthias**, regimental surgeon's mate – overworked, practical, willing
  to try what works and unable to say why it does.
- **Pater Ignaz**, Jesuit professor of mathematics at the university – has read
  Schott and Kircher, owns a telescope and an air pump he has never used in public.
- **Hans Georg Pichler**, journeyman gun founder at the arsenal – can cast, bore and
  turn; thinks in bronze and powder.
- **Mehmed**, a captured Ottoman sapper – knows how the other side digs; whether he
  is treated as a source, a prisoner or a man is up to the player.

## Evaluation focus
- Disease killed more defenders than the enemy. Did the player find the causes
  (water, latrines, crowding, lice) and get anything changed under siege conditions?
- Did they prepare in the three weeks before the gates closed?
- Which of the era's half-known ideas (vacuum, microscope, pendulum, probability)
  did they connect to, and did they reach the people abroad who could use them?
- What did their help do to the war, and did they think about it?
