- **start date:** 12 March 1490 (adjustable)
- **hook:** accident (default) or mission from the future (option below)

## Premise
Florence at the height of the Renaissance. Lorenzo de' Medici rules a republic that
isn't one. Ficino translates Plato, Botticelli paints, the boy Michelangelo carves in
the Medici garden. And in the narrow streets of the Oltrarno, the plague smoulders
again. A Dominican friar named Savonarola has just begun to preach that God's
punishment is coming.

## Mission from the future (optional hook)
The message reads: *"Plague will kill thousands in Florence this year. Stop it. You
will be returned in one year."* (The outbreak in this campaign is fictional in scale
but typical for the era.)

## Opening scene
The player wakes on the stones of the Ponte Vecchio at dawn. Butchers and tanners
are opening their shops on the bridge; offal goes straight into the Arno. A boy
stares at the player's shoes. In a pocket: the message.

## Arc (loose, react to the player)
1. **Arrival** – find shelter, money, someone who listens. An innkeeper in the
   Oltrarno, a curious apothecary, or a friar who suspects witchcraft.
2. **First signs** – fever cases in San Frediano. Officials blame bad air; some
   blame strangers (possibly the player).
3. **Access** – via Ficino (a physician-philosopher, curious about the occult and
   about plague) or Benivieni (a practical physician who opens corpses) to the
   Medici circle.
4. **The test** – Lorenzo, sceptical but pragmatic, gives the player a quarter, a
   budget or a workshop for a fixed time. Results count.
5. **Opposition** – physicians whose remedies fail, Savonarola's followers, a
   rival who wants the player's phone.
6. **Branch outs** – the player can pursue other technologies: lenses and a
   microscope with the spectacle makers, a pump for a mine, a trip to Milan to meet
   Leonardo, a semaphore line to Pisa.

## Medium-difficulty hint examples (incidental, never highlighted)
- A washerwoman complains that her neighbour's bedding was sold, not burned.
- A grain merchant mentions his warehouse cats have been "lazy this year, too many
  rats" (do not repeat this hint in a more obvious form).
- At Careggi, Ficino quotes Varro's "animalia minuta" in an aside about swamps.
- A spectacle maker brags that his strongest lens makes a flea look like a dog.

## People specific to this campaign
- **Bartolomeo**, innkeeper near Santo Spirito – practical, greedy, loyal if paid.
- **Monna Lisabetta**, washerwoman in San Frediano – knows every house, distrusts
  doctors.
- **Maestro Iacopo**, spectacle maker on Via dei Calzaiuoli – ambitious, wants a
  product nobody else has.
- **Ser Niccolò**, notary of the health officials – keeps lists of the dead; useful
  data, if the player asks the right questions.
- **Fra Domenico**, a Dominican follower of Savonarola – suspicious of the player's
  "devilish" objects.
(These are fictional; real people are listed in the era file.)

## Evaluation focus
- Did the player identify the real transmission routes?
- Did they build proof (microscope, counting deaths by place and time) or only
  rely on authority?
- Which cheap measures (isolation, burning bedding, killing rats/fleas, soap, clean
  water) did they push through?
