# Campaign: Florence 1347 – Before the Plague

- **id:** `florence-1347`
- **era guide:** `techtree/generated/europe-1347-florence.md` (compact fallback: `techtree/generated/europe-1347-florence/briefing.md`; source: `techtree/eras/europe-1347-florence.yaml`)
- **start date:** 15 October 1347 (adjustable)
- **hook:** mission from the future (default for this campaign) or accident
- **recommended duration:** one year

## Premise
The same city as in *Florence 1490*, 143 years earlier and without a printing press.
Florence is worn out: the harvest of 1346 failed and thousands died in the famine
of spring 1347, and the great banks of the Bardi and Peruzzi have collapsed. In the same weeks,
far to the south, Genoese galleys from the Black Sea put in at Messina with dying
crews. By spring the sickness will be in Florence, and by autumn about half the city
will be dead.

This is the game's hygiene and epidemic test in its purest form. The player knows
what is coming and when. Nobody else does, and nobody has a reason to believe a
stranger.

## Mission from the future
The message reads: *"The great mortality reaches this city in the spring. Half of
them will die. You will be returned in one year."* The player is not immune and has
no cure. Whatever is in their pockets (a few tablets, a lighter, a phone) is all the
future they have.

## Opening scene
Dawn at the grain market of Orsanmichele. Sellers set out their bins under the
loggia; buyers argue over prices that have not come down since the famine of the
spring, when the commune baked bread in its own ovens and handed it out by ticket.
The air smells of wet wool and smoke. A notary's boy looks up from his wax tablet at
the player's clothes. In a pocket: the message.

## Arc (loose, react to the player)
1. **Arrival in an exhausted city** – food, shelter, a patron. Bread is dear and
   strangers are suspect.
2. **Rumour** – merchants' letters from Sicily, then from Pisa (January 1348): a
   pestilence that kills in three days. Physicians speak of corrupted air and the
   conjunction of the planets.
3. **A hearing** – a guild consul, a friar, a physician or a prior of the commune
   listens. The priors serve only two months, so every ally has to be won again.
4. **Winter of preparation** – the only time measures cost little: watching the roads
   from Pisa, cleaning, rats and fleas, isolating houses, clean water, burial plans,
   stocks of grain and soap. Without printing, each measure must be carried by
   decree, sermon, guild and word of mouth.
5. **The mortality** – March to September 1348. Shops and courts close, the rich
   leave for their villas, priests and notaries die or flee. What the player built
   in winter either holds or does not. The player can fall ill.
6. **After** – a city with half its people, sudden inheritances, high wages, and a
   commune that decides to found a university.

## Hard choices
- **Flee or stay.** Leaving for the hills is the best personal advice of the age and
  works. It also abandons the mission.
- **"Something invisible in the water."** In 1348–49 Jews across Europe are accused
  of poisoning wells and murdered. A careless explanation of contagion can feed that
  accusation. The game master lets the consequence follow from what the player
  actually says and to whom (see `modules/content-guidelines.md`), and never has the
  player's words alone cause a massacre without warning signs.
- **Authority.** The fastest route to mass behaviour change is the pulpit. Using it
  means framing measures in terms a friar can preach, and owing the Church a favour.

## Medium-difficulty hint examples (incidental, never highlighted)
- A wool carder mentions that the sick house next door was emptied and its bedding
  sold at the used-clothes market.
- A grain official complains that the stores at Orsanmichele are "alive at night".
- A letter read aloud in a warehouse: in Messina those who carried the dead out fell
  ill, those who shut themselves in the hills did not.
- A physician remarks in passing that tanners and those who work in bad smells seem
  no worse off than anyone else.

## People specific to this campaign (fictional; real people are in the era file)
- **Monna Tessa**, widow who runs a lodging house near Orsanmichele – counts every
  loaf, knows who is ill before the physicians do.
- **Ser Lapo**, notary to the grain officials – keeps the ration lists of the spring; the best
  population data in the city, if the player thinks to ask.
- **Maestro Bindo**, physician trained at Bologna – learned, honest, certain that the
  cause is in the air and the stars.
- **Fra Ruggieri**, Dominican preacher at Santa Maria Novella – a voice that reaches
  thousands; sees pestilence as punishment and the stranger as a possible prophet or
  a possible fraud.
- **Neri**, apprentice soap boiler – practical, poor, the first to try something
  because he has nothing to lose.

## Evaluation focus
- Did the player identify the real transmission routes (rat fleas, lice and fleas on
  people and cloth, coughing in the pneumonic form) or stay with "bad air" under a
  new name?
- Did they use the winter, or start when people were already dying?
- Which measures did they get adopted, through which channel, and did they survive
  the change of priors every two months?
- How did they explain invisible causes without printing, a microscope or proof, and
  did their words put anyone in danger?
- Counterfactual: how many of the city's dead could cheap measures have saved? Be
  honest about uncertainty; even perfect measures would not have stopped the plague
  entirely.
